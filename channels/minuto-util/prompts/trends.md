historical event in under a minute, over archive photos and footage.

For now the channel tells Brazil's history only. A pick must be one of:
- An event in Brazil, from before Cabral (the first peoples, the sambaquis, Marajoara) to 25 years ago.
- A war or treaty Brazil took part in, in South America or beyond: the Cisplatine, Platine and Paraguayan wars, the
  Acre War, the Treaty of Tordesillas.
- The World Wars told through Brazil's part: the ships sunk off the coast, the navy in 1918, the FEB in Italy.

Spread the picks over colonial Brazil (1500 to 1822), the Empire (1822 to 1889) and the Republic (1889 to 25 years
ago), at least two from each, and over every region of the country, not only Rio and São Paulo. Revolts, wars,
expeditions, inventions, disasters, sport, art, religion (told as history) and great figures are all welcome; at most
two picks from the same war.

You also get what Brazil is talking about today: the top news headlines, the most searched terms and the most read
Wikipedia pages. Use it only as a loose inspiration, never a reason to stay in recent times: a story about an
election can lead to the Republic's first vote or the Revolution of 1930; a football final to the Maracanazo.
Searches are often football fixtures, match results, celebrities, lotteries or product launches:
skip those unless one has a real historical story behind it. Each pick's `trend` names the story it ties to, or the
series when it ties to none.

Rules for every pick:
- A single real event with its own English Wikipedia article, written exactly as the article's title (for a person,
  the article on one moment of their life when there is one, like "Assassination of Julius Caesar").
- It happened at least 25 years ago. `year` is negative before Christ (-480 for 480 BC), never 0; for an approximate
  date use the year historians usually give.
- Never the current news itself, never a living politician, current party, candidate or campaign as the subject, and
  no opinion: the channel is neutral and only tells what happened; religion is told as history, respectfully.
- Not centered on gore, a recent tragedy, or a crime against a private person.
- Prefer events with good pictures: old paintings, engravings, maps, manuscripts, or photos and film after about
  1860, and a clear story with a surprising detail.
- Do not pick anything in the "already made" list, and lean away from the periods and wars it already has many of.

keeping every rule; an idea about another country becomes Brazil's side of it, when there is a true one. A Portuguese
